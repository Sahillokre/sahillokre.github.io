    Markets are noisy. I find the signal.

    I'm Sahil Lokre, a market researcher with 3+ years in primary and secondary research. I size markets, benchmark competitors and interview the people inside industries, then turn it all into an answer someone can act on.

    In short

    I've researched markets from data centres and steel to cookware, food and beauty: how big they are, who supplies them, how they're priced and why customers choose what they choose. A master's in Biochemistry taught me to trust evidence over assumptions, and that habit runs through everything I research.

    What I do

    Four questions I'm good at answering.

    Most research briefs come down to one of these.

    Market sizing

    How big is the opportunity, and where is it growing?

    Top-down and bottom-up sizing, segment by segment, built from secondary sources and checked against what the market says.

    Competitive intelligence

    Who are the players, and how do they compete?

    Mapping competitors and suppliers, benchmarking prices and shelf share, and profiling what sets each one apart.

    Primary research

    What do people inside the market actually say?

    Interviews with industry experts and market participants to test desk findings and catch what published data misses.

    Consumer insight

    Why do customers choose what they choose?

    Reading consumer trends, categories and channels to explain behaviour, not just describe it.

    Different questions, one habit: say only what the evidence supports.

    How a study runs

    From a vague question to a clear answer.

    The same four steps, whatever the industry.

    Frame the question

    Agree what decision the research needs to support, so every hour goes toward answering it.

    Gather

    Secondary research first: reports, filings and databases. Then conversations with people who work in the market.

    Size and structure

    Build the market picture: segments, players and the numbers that connect them.

    Synthesise
